## 2. Rulings I made that the brief and the contract did not

Each says what would overturn it.

### 2.1 The command ring is 4 MiB, not 8, and one record is capped at 2 MiB, not 4

`CONTRACT-P5 §5` and `Config.h`'s `MOBILEGL_IPC_RING_MB` comment both say "8 MiB caps one record
at 4 MiB". **That arithmetic treats the whole segment as ring bytes and does not subtract the
control page.** `Ring.h:11-13` puts `RingControl` at the HEAD of SEG_CMD, it is 4096 bytes by
`static_assert`, and `RingProducer` requires a POWER-OF-TWO capacity because the indexing is a
mask (`Ring.cpp:89-103`). So an 8 MiB SEG_CMD leaves 8 MiB − 4096, and the largest power of two
that fits is **4 MiB** → `MaxRecordBytes() == 2 MiB`.

I took the deviation in the direction that is safe: **the announced `SegmentRef.sizeBytes` stays
the four pinned numbers** (8 / 32 / 8 MiB + 256 KiB, `ProtocolSmokeTest.cpp:72`) and the ring
inside SEG_CMD is half of its segment. R-10's obligation is to PROVE no record approaches the
cap, and a lower cap makes that proof fire earlier and louder rather than later and silently.

Two alternatives, both rejected and why:
* announce `sizeBytes = 4096 + 8 MiB` — breaks the four sizes the brief says to copy and that
  `ProtocolSmokeTest.cpp:72` pins, and is the number a P6 peer has to `mmap`;
* move `RingControl` out of SEG_CMD — needs a fifth `SegmentRef` that `Welcome` does not have.

**SEG_STAGE is unaffected**: `RingControl` holds TWO cursor triples and the stage triple is the
second, so SEG_STAGE has no control page of its own and 32 MiB is already a power of two.
SEG_EVENT gets its own control page (a third cursor set would resize the 4096-byte shared page),
so its 256 KiB yields a 128 KiB ring.

**Overturned by**: a decision that `SegmentRef.sizeBytes` may carry `ring + one page`; or a
`RingProducer` that accepts a non-power-of-two capacity.

### 2.2 `CapsSnapshot` gains `callMask` and `backendType`; `Hello`/`Welcome` gain `abiFingerprint`

Table 0 deletes four `CapsSnapshot` fields and **names no carrier for `MGPCaps::CallMask`** — but
R-8/C-4 makes `CallMask` the *only* legal client-side source of "does the server consume this
family", and `dynamicParameters` is the image of `DynamicBackendParameters`, which `CallMask` is
not a member of. Without a carrier, R-8 is unimplementable for a second time. Likewise
`CapsMirror::Backend()` must return the **server's** `BackendType` and nothing on the control
plane carried it (`Hello.backendType` is the client's request).

Four appends, all safe: the deleted four were the LAST four fields, so no surviving field moved a
vtable slot, and every addition is at the end.

| table | added | why |
|---|---|---|
| `CapsSnapshot` | `callMask: ulong` | R-8/C-4's consumer mask + the nine `MGPCapBit`s |
| `CapsSnapshot` | `backendType: uint` | `CapsMirror::Backend()`; frontend branches take a wrong arm rather than fail on a value they do not know |
| `Hello` | `abiFingerprint: ulong` | table 0's ABI agreement; `Hello` already carried the string, not the sizes |
| `Welcome` | `buildFingerprint: string`, `abiFingerprint: ulong` | the server's half of the same assertion; the string is only so the Fatal can name both builds |

**Overturned by**: folding `CallMask` into the head of the `dynamicParameters` blob (cheaper on the
wire, worse to read), or carrying the server's backend type inside `rendererInfo`.

### 2.3 The SERVER creates all four segments; the client attaches

`Welcome` announces all four `SegmentRef`s and `Welcome` is server → client, and
`ServerSession::Accept`'s own doc comment says "maps the four segments". "Client-owned" in
`protocol.fbs`'s comments is about who WRITES a segment, not who allocates it. Under `inproc` the
client attaches to the same mapping under its own ledger role; under `spawn` that call becomes
`ShmSegment::Adopt` of the fds the server passed by SCM_RIGHTS, which is why nothing below
`SessionSegments` knows which it was.

### 2.4 Reply pool geometry: **8 slots of 1 MiB**

`ReplyPool::SlotBytes()` is "sized from the scenario's largest read rather than guessed", and the
trade is the opposite way round from the obvious one: **while the verb barrier holds, the in-flight
depth is exactly ONE**, so every extra slot buys nothing except a smaller maximum answer. 8 MiB /
8 = 1 MiB, which covers a 512×512 RGBA8 `ReadPixels`. The slot count must be a power of two (the
addressing is a mask, and a division on the apply thread in the reply path of every blocking call
is not free). **Overturned by**: t1's scenarios needing a bigger single read, which is spelled as a
*lower* slot count, not a higher one. P9 re-chooses the whole geometry when it makes the pool
asynchronous.

### 2.5 `ReplyPool::PostReply` is implemented inside v1's `PipeApplier.cpp`

The SEG_REPLY slot pool is s1's deliverable (BRIEF §5) but c0 declared the class in
`Server/PipeApplier.h`, which is v1's file. The resolution: the addressing lives once, in
`Transport/ReplySlot.h`, which the CLIENT reads the same slots back through; `PostReply`'s body is
a two-line delegation that rebuilds the view from `ReplyPool`'s existing four members. **v1's
header is untouched, `ReplyPool`'s members are untouched, and every `PipeApplier::*` body is
untouched** — the diff in v1's file is one `#include` and one function body.

### 2.6 The consumer mask and the cap bits are setters with derived defaults, and v1 owns the answer

**Nothing in the tree produces `MGPCaps::CallMask`** — it is declared at `MGPipeTypes.h:133` and
written by nobody. So `ServerSession` takes both halves through setters:

* `SetConsumedSubsystems(mask)`. The default derives from the only registration that exists:
  `PipeFill.cpp:920`'s `P4aFamilyHasItsConsumer` says every P4a family hangs off
  `MGPipeGetResourceOps()` and that there is deliberately "no per-family registration to add", so
  the default is `kMGPipeSubsystemsMigratedAtP2` when that table is null and
  `kMGPipeSubsystemsMigratedAtP4a` when it is not. **This is a default, not a ruling — v1 should
  confirm or replace it.** Publishing a bit the server does not consume is ID-39's shape from the
  other side: the client emits and nothing applies.
* `SetCapabilityBits(bits)`. The default sets only `kCapResidentSubData`, from
  `MGPipeResourceOpsHaveSubDataResident()`. Every other bit is **0**, deliberately: R-15's
  cross-cutting rule is that a cap bit set on a guess answers "supported" for a path that does not
  exist, which is how a split lane produces a plausible picture for the wrong reason.
  `kCapNeedsHostIndexBytes` / `kCapNeedsHostUboBytes` stay 0 for all of P5 by ruling.

### 2.7 Doorbell placement — contract §3.9, implemented as ruled

The two accessors stay **off** `ITransport`. `ServerSession::ConsumerDoorbell()` /
`ProducerDoorbell()` resolve the pair from `ITransport::Role()` in **one switch in one file**, and
P6 adds one arm there rather than two virtuals every transport has to find a home for. Everything
else takes `SessionProducer&` / `SessionConsumer&`, which already hold the right two references,
so no call site outside those two functions knows which bell is which.

## 8.2 The flag-space collisions

`RingRecordHeader::flags` is read in exactly two places of mine, and there are **three** colliding
bits, not one — w1's `kReplySlot ≡ kRecVarTail` is the least dangerous of them:

| bit | `MGPipeCallFlags` | `RingRecordFlags` | consequence if a call's flags reach the header |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | `kVarTail` | `kRecPad` | **worst**: `Pop` skipped every var-tail record as a wrap filler, silently, record lost, nothing logged |
| 3 | `kHostSpan` | `kRecBorrowSlot` | my `ApplyOne` stops reclaiming ring bytes behind it — the producer wedges on the first full ring |
| 4 | `kReplySlot` | `kRecVarTail` | a blocking call reads as having a tail it does not have |

Translating is the encoder's job, but neither of my two readers now depends on it:

* **`RingConsumer::Pop` requires a filler to carry `kRecPad` AND `kind == kRingPadRecordKind`.** A
  genuine filler always does (Reserve writes it); a call record always carries a real opcode,
  because the catalogue starts at 1. One comparison turns a lost record into a delivered one.
  `SessionTest.ARecordWearingThePadBitIsDeliveredRatherThanEatenAsAFiller`.
* **`SessionConsumer` counts and names every `kRecBorrowSlot` sighting** (`BorrowedRecordsSeen()`,
  one `MGLOG_E` on the first). P5 implements no borrowed slots and is ruled to produce no host spans
  either, so the bit arriving *is* the collision; the conservative arm is still taken, but a
  producer that then wedges has a line saying why.
  `SessionTest.ABorrowSlotSightingIsCountedRatherThanJustActedOn`.
* Four `static_assert`s in `ServerSession.cpp` — the nearest `.cpp` that legally sees both headers,
  since nothing under `Transport/` may reach `MobileGL/Includes.h` — pin all three collisions and
  the two bits that agree, so a renumber of either enum is a build break rather than news. Ring.h's
  `RingRecordFlags` carries the table in prose.
