## 1. E2's negative control — why it stayed green, and what replaced it

### The finding

`MOBILEGL_IPC_E2_DROP_CLEAR` was not broken. All four of the joint's hypotheses were tested and
the answer is the first one.

* **Is the knob read on that path?** Yes. `BuildRemoteEmitTable()` reads it and the WARN is in the
  library's own log. Added here: the *count*. With the knob armed the library now prints, once per
  Present, `MGPipe: E2 control armed - drop-draw=0 drop-clear=1, 29 records dropped on the wire
  (draw=0 clear=29), frame 29`. **29 Clear records were dropped** — the knob is on the live path
  and the record never reached the ring.
* **Does it drop before or after the frame's first draw?** Before, and it does not matter: it drops
  *every* clear in every frame.
* **Does the harness compare a frame the clear never affects?** Effectively yes, and this is the
  cause. `apitrace dump --calls=0-31249 openra.trace` over the replayed range counts
  **30 `glClear`, 30 `glXSwapBuffers`, 788 `glDrawArrays`, 752 `glBufferSubData` and ZERO
  `glDrawElements`** (which is also why OpenRA raises no `Fatal{UnmigratedVerb, "DrawElements"}`).
  The snapshot frame issues its clear at call **30197** and then covers the surface four times over
  with a terrain layer — `glDrawArrays(GL_TRIANGLES, first=56064, count=16128)` ×4 under a scissor
  of `-24,-24 688×528` over a 640×480 surface — before the target call **31249**. A frame that
  overdraws every pixel it clears has a picture that does not depend on the clear.

So the knob's *observable* is invisible to this trace. That is R-16's exact defect — a gate that
cannot go red for its own reason — and it is a property of the workload, not of the emitter.

### What shipped

`MOBILEGL_IPC_E2_DROP_DRAW=1` (`MG_Remote/Client/EmitTables.cpp`, beside the clear drop; same
shape — `BeforeDrawVerb()` still runs, only the record is skipped). The golden *is* made of that
geometry, so it is the honest "the wire carried the frame" control.

`MOBILEGL_IPC_E2_DROP_CLEAR` is **kept, not retired**: it drops a real record, it now publishes the
count it dropped, and it is observable wherever the picture depends on the clear
(`ClearThenReadPixelsScenario` is the reduced path's target A). What it is no longer allowed to be
is E2's retrace control. Both knobs are still `getenv` and not `MG_Config::Ipc` — `Config.h` is
c0's; the request to adopt them into `IpcTable` stands (c1-v2 §10 debt 6, unchanged).

`scripts/ci/retrace_drop_draw_control.sh` is the lane's control (new file; the body of a new step in
`.github/workflows/test.yml`'s `retrace-split` job, beside the existing pull-library control).
**It never accepts a bare threshold failure.** Three guards, each a different way for "the picture
went red" to be somebody else's red:

1. a **non-empty selection**, counted before the run (ID-46 finding 8(b)'s hole);
2. the **SSIM actually fell** — `ssim=` and `ssimThreshold=` are parsed out of the retrace's own
   summary and compared with `awk`, so a loader failure, a missing fixture, a timeout or a `Fatal{`
   (all non-zero, all not this red) are refused;
3. the **library said it dropped something** — its own `MGPipe: E2 control armed … N records
   dropped on the wire` line from the replay's log, with **N > 0**. Without this, a knob that was
   never read would redden the picture for some other reason and pass.

## 2. R-10's proof obligation — the maximum record bytes, published

`PipeWireEncoder::MaxRecordBytesSeen()` existed with no consumer outside the codec unit cases, so
BRIEF §8 item 3 had no measurement (joint-v1 §5, "NO MEASUREMENT"). Three channels now carry it:

* **the `MGPipe stats:` line** — a new push-only `PipeStats::Gauge` family, published by the client
  at the frame boundary: `maxrec=<bytes> maxcap=<cap> ringwraps=<n> ringpads=<n> ringwaits=<n>`,
  inside the existing `#if MOBILEGL_PIPE_PUSH` block (G1 is intact, see §5). They are **run totals on
  a windowed line**, deliberately and documented: a windowed maximum reads 0 in every window that
  did not contain the biggest record, which is a proof obligation that cannot fail. They are
  published **before** the Present record is emitted — placed after it, the first summary line of
  every run read `maxrec=0 maxcap=0`, and a zero meaning "not published yet" is printed in the same
  shape as a zero meaning "nothing crossed the wire".
* **every split private log**, at `ClientSession::Stop`, with no `MOBILEGL_PIPE_STATS` needed —
  only 2 of the 21 split entries set it, and R-10 is about the phase, not about two counting lanes:
  `MG_Remote client: wire ledger: maxrec=784 maxrecop=SetVertexAttribDefaults cap=4194304 (0.0% of
  RingProducer::MaxRecordBytes, half of a 8388608 byte SEG_CMD) cmdbytes=4040 ringwraps=0 ringpads=0
  ringwaits=0 emitseq=48`.
* **the row name**, because R-10 makes the integrator choose between early chunking and a bigger
  ring and that is a decision about a record *family*. A trace replay never reaches
  `ClientSession::Stop` (measured: the OpenRA lane's library log simply ends), so a stats-enabled run
  also logs `MGPipe: wire ledger: new maximum record - maxrec=… maxrecop=… cap=…` whenever the
  maximum grows — bounded by the number of distinct maxima, one in a whole OpenRA replay.

## 3. E3(e) — the SmallRing lane proved nothing, and now it does

### The finding, measured before anything was asserted

A whole split scenario writes **about 4 KiB** into SEG_CMD (`emitseq` 22–71, `cmdbytes` 4 040–4 904,
`maxrec` 784). The lane's `MOBILEGL_IPC_RING_MB=1` is **250× the traffic**. The head never came near
its wrap boundary, so `DirectGLES.Split.SmallRing.*` and `DirectGLES.Split.*` were the same run under
two names — exactly what ID-53's own comment and t1-v1's debt warned about
("WHAT THIS LANE DOES NOT YET ASSERT").

### A second finding, which changed what is asserted

The first cut asserted the **kRecPad** count — R-9's "a pad record does not advance seq, both sides
must skip it and count again". Driving 1 310 824 bytes through the 1 MiB ring produced **one and a
half trips round the ring and ZERO pads**. The reason is arithmetic, not a defect: a workload whose
records repeat at a uniform stride that *divides* a power-of-two capacity lands on the boundary
exactly, every time. A gate on the pad count would have been a gate on the sizes in the record
catalogue, red today and green the day one of them changed.

So **three** counters are kept and only one is load-bearing:

* `ringwraps=` — the head crossed a multiple of the capacity (the ring went round). **Asserted**: it
  is guaranteed once the workload writes more bytes than the ring holds.
* `ringpads=` — a record straddled the boundary and got a filler. **Recorded**, not asserted.
* `ringwaits=` — SEG_STAGE allocations that only fitted after the encoder reclaimed what the server
  had retired: the producer's progress depended on `retiredSeq`. **This is P5's only real
  back-pressure event**, and the command ring has none by construction — the verb barrier makes
  `EmitAndWait` wait for `appliedSeq` after *every* record (R-1), so at most one record is ever in
  flight on SEG_CMD and a full command ring is not a wait but `Fatal{RingOverrun}`. Publishing a
  "command-ring waits" counter that can only ever read zero-or-dead would be a decoration.

### What shipped

`TriangleScenario.TheSameVboAndVaoRedrawAcrossAFrameBoundary`, in the SmallRing lane only, drives
ordinary GL (clear + VBO-backed draw, no readback — a readback is a SEG_REPLY round trip per
iteration) until the producer has written more than 1.25 MiB, then asserts. Nothing touches the ring
directly: the loop's only input is the producer's own head cursor through
`Harness/SplitRuntimePeek`, and its only output is GL the scenario already issues. The assertion
prints the **denominator with the count**, because "0 wraps" is a defect after 1.25 MiB through a
1 MiB ring and a tautology after 4 KiB.

Measured, green: `cmdbytes=1314880 ringwraps=1 ringpads=0 ringwaits=1 emitseq=24323`, cap 524 288.
Cost: that one entry goes from 0.31 s to 0.68 s.

### Red once

`~/w7/p5-x2-e3e-redcheck.sh 8` re-runs **that entry's own command and environment** with
`MOBILEGL_IPC_RING_MB` raised from the lane's floor of 1 to the default 8, nothing else changed.
The assertion names the missing wrap:

> exit gate E3(e) - 1310824 bytes were written into a 8388608 byte SEG_CMD and the producer's head
> NEVER WENT ROUND: no wrap, so this entry exercised exactly what the default lane exercises and the
> word SmallRing in its name asserts nothing…

followed by its own explanation (the denominator). `…redcheck.sh 1` is green on the same binary.
The order is deliberate: the missing wrap is the gate, the byte count is the reason, and an `ASSERT`
on the denominator would have printed only the reason — which is how the first version of this
red-check read, and why it is written this way round.

> `MGPipe: persistent-map push disabled - MOBILEGL_IPC_PERSISTENT_BLOCK_KB=0 is exit gate E3(a)'s

> pushed, so the server draws whatever bytes last crossed by some other route. A lane that stays

## 5. An incidental finding: the control-smoke red-check never perturbed the split control

On this base, `scripts/ci/redcheck_control_smoke_test.sh` looked for an evidence check on a line
starting with `if ! `. The split control's has been `elif ! tr -s … | grep -qE "${evidence}"` since it
was written, so the perturbation found **zero** checks in it, the Python `SystemExit` was not checked
by the caller, and the run went on to smoke-test the **unperturbed** controls and print
"RED-CHECK FAILED: the controls accept an unrelated failure again and the smoke test still passed" —
a true sentence about a perturbation that never happened. It failed *safe*, so nothing was ever
silently proved, but the control it was most about was never exercised. Reproduced on
`p5/joint@e61d0012` before the fix.

Fixed here, and extended: the perturbation now accepts `elif`, keeps the keyword when it rewrites the
line (turning an `elif` into an `if` splits the chain and leaves a dangling `fi`), aborts loudly when
it does not apply, and reverts **seven** checks across three controls — including E1's private-log
read and E3(a)'s new one, without which the smoke test's "unrelated failure" case never flips,
because the other guards catch it. `P5_T1_CONTROL_SMOKE_REDCHECK_OK`: 11 passed / 5 failed, red on
exactly the five cases the evidence checks pin.

This file may also have been touched by `p5/j0`'s rounds 2–3 (`cc20de37`), which the joint did not
merge. **Merge care is needed there.**

## 8. For the integrator

1. **`MOBILEGL_IPC_E2_DROP_CLEAR` is kept but is no longer E2's retrace control.** If you would rather
   retire it, the one thing that would be lost is a cheap perturbation for scenarios whose picture
   *does* depend on the clear; nothing in CI depends on it today.
2. **Both E2 knobs are still `getenv`.** Adopting them into `MG_Config::Ipc` is a `Config.h` edit,
   i.e. c0's. c1 asked once (c1-v2 §10 item 6); x2 asks again, now that there are two of them.
3. **`ringwaits` on SEG_CMD is structurally zero while R-1's barrier is armed.** The published
   `ringwaits` is the SEG_STAGE reclaim wait, and it is non-zero on real workloads
   (OpenRA: 11). When the barrier retires family by family in P6, a command-ring wait becomes
   reachable and wants its own counter.
4. **The SmallRing lane's ring floor is `MOBILEGL_IPC_RING_MB=1` and that is 250× the reduced path's
   traffic.** x2 closed the gap by making the workload bigger, which is what was available; the
   alternative — a KiB-granularity ring knob — is a `Config.h`/`ConfigLoader.cpp` change and was not
   taken unilaterally. Worth a ruling before P6 adds lanes.
5. **R-10 is closed and comfortable**: 784 bytes against a 4 MiB cap, row `SetVertexAttribDefaults`.
   The MEASUREMENTS row for BRIEF §8 item 3 can be written from §2's table.
6. **`redcheck_control_smoke_test.sh` collides with `p5/j0@cc20de37`** if j0's later rounds touched
   it. Take x2's version's *matcher* (the `elif`/keyword fix and the abort-on-no-op) whichever way the
   merge goes — without it that script has never perturbed the split control.
7. **The local gate is not updated.** `~/w7/notes/tools/wsl_p5_gate*.sh` live outside the repo and are
   shared; the drop-draw control is wired into CI only. One line beside the existing
   `retrace_pull_library_control.sh` invocation would add it, and the gate's E2 section is the right
   place.
8. **A retrace process never reaches `ClientSession::Stop`** — measured, the OpenRA lane's library log
   just ends — so the teardown ledger is absent from retrace logs by design, and the stats-enabled
   "new maximum record" line is what covers that lane.
